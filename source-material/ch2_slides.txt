Figure 2.17: Spatial and Temporal Summation
Graded potentials are combined at the axon hillock in two ways:
Spatial summation
combines graded potentials occurring simultaneously at different locations on the dendrites and cell body.
Temporal summation
combines graded potentials arriving a short time apart, from either the same or separate inputs.

===== SLIDE 18 =====
Regulating Synaptic Activity
Garrett, Brain & Behavior, 7e. © 2027 Sage Publishing.
18
--- SMARTART ---
The synapses described so far are referred to as
axodendritic
and
axosomatic
synapses.
Their terminals connect to dendrites and cell bodies.
At
axoaxonic
synapses, a third neuron releases transmitter onto the terminals of the presynaptic neuron.
The result is
presynaptic excitation or presynaptic inhibition
, which increases or decreases the presynaptic neuron’s release of neurotransmitter onto the postsynaptic neuron.
Neurons also regulate their own synaptic activity in two ways.
An autoreceptor on the presynaptic terminal senses the amount of transmitter in the cleft; if the amount is excessive, the presynaptic neuron reduces its output
When there are unusual increases or decreases in neurotransmitter release, postsynaptic receptors change their sensitivity or even their numbers to compensate
Glial cells also contribute to the regulation of synaptic activity.
Surround the synapse and prevent neurotransmitter from spreading to other synapse
Remove neurotransmitter from the synaptic cleft and recycle it for the neuron’s reuse

===== SLIDE 22 =====
Interpreting Neural Information: Neural Codes
Garrett, Brain & Behavior, 7e. © 2027 Sage Publishing.
22
--- SMARTART ---
Neurons vary the intervals between spikes, producing bursts of varying lengths, and the bursts can be separated by different intervals.
The brain uses these variations in firing patterns to form a code that is used to interpret input from the body and the world.
However, this coding is not sufficient to carry the complex information involved in communication in the brain.

===== SLIDE 23 =====
Interpreting Neural Information: Neural Networks
Neural networks
are groups of neurons that function together to carry out more complex processes.
Sometimes these networks involve a relatively small number of neurons in a localized area carrying out a single process (e.g., discriminating the color of objects).
Sometimes these neural networks combine the activity of widespread brain areas to engage in more complex tasks (e.g., language processing, depth perception, consciousness).
Garrett, Brain & Behavior, 7e. © 2027 Sage Publishing.
